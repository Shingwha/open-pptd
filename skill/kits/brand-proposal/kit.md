# Kit · brand-proposal

**System:** `editorial` (see `../../references/design.md` §3).
**Audience:** brand owner, marketing lead, creative jury, client decision
maker.
**Reader task:** believe the concept, see the system, and approve the direction.

## Narrative

```
cover → opportunity/insight → concept → visual system → campaign/applications
→ proof → closing/ask
```

The deck sells a point of view. It may be expressive, but every loud page must
be followed by a page that proves or explains. One concept, one visual rule,
one motif — consistency is the craft.

## Required pages

| Page | Job |
|---|---|
| Cover | Subject, proposal type, author/date; one strong visual anchor. |
| Opportunity | The cultural/business opening, audience insight, brand problem. |
| Concept | The idea in one sentence plus three pillars and the visual rationale. |
| Visual system | Wordmark usage, palette, type, grid, imagery, motion principles. |
| Campaign / applications | The idea across 2–3 touchpoints, with real formats. |
| Proof | Audience test, benchmark, pilot result, or credible precedent. |
| Closing / ask | Restate the concept, the ask, the next step, contact. |

Optional: team/credits, timeline, budget, appendix with production specs. Keep
the pitch to the idea; production detail goes last.

## Density and type

- Body 15–18 px; captions 10–11 px; page titles 36–48 px; statement/cover type
  64–120 px.
- Rhythm: statement page → evidence page → system page → application page. Not
  every page is a poster.
- One image per page; no photo collages. Images are expression unless labelled
  as evidence.
- One motif, repeated with state changes. No second motif.

## Evidence rules

- Brand assets (logo, VI, product shots) come from the user or official source;
  never redraw or generate them.
- Audience/test data is evidence: cite the method, n, and date. No fake quotes,
  fake awards, or invented case results.
- Mockups are labelled as mockups; they are not proof of performance.
- Generated imagery is expression only, with consent and provenance. Never
  generate a person as a testimonial or a product as fact.

## Do

- Make the concept sentence memorable and specific.
- Show the system on real formats, not abstract swatches alone.
- Let type do the work; use scale and whitespace as materials.
- Keep one accent and one motif; repeat them with discipline.
- End with a clear ask: approve, fund, or proceed to the next gate.

## Don't

- Don't use generic AI gradients, glassmorphism, rounded card stacks, or
  decorative blobs.
- Don't mix two visual languages or two accent colors.
- Don't use stock photos as evidence or fake client logos.
- Don't apply editorial styling to a business review or an academic deck.
- Don't make every page loud; the quiet pages make the loud ones work.

## Anti-homogenization

- The page order is a pitch logic, not a template. Drop or reorder pages when
  the story needs it.
- Alternate loud and quiet pages; no two consecutive pages share a composition.
- Use at least four recipes in a ten-page deck; never run three full-bleed
  statement pages in a row.
- `fragments/` are single-page geometry references. Never assemble them into a
  fixed deck and never copy the kit wholesale.
