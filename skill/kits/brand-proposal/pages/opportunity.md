# Page recipe · opportunity

**Purpose:** show why this brand, this audience, this moment. The proposal is
dead if the opportunity is generic.

**Reader task:** recognize a real tension or opening they have not resolved.

## Slots

1. Title: the opportunity as a tension ("A premium product sold like a
   commodity").
2. Evidence: 2–3 observations (audience shift, category convention, cultural
   signal), each sourced.
3. Audience insight: one sentence in the audience's own language.
4. Brand problem: what the brand must change or claim.
5. Why now: the trigger or window.

## Variants

**A. Tension → insight** — left: the observation; right: the insight in 24–32
px. The default.

**B. Cultural timeline** — 3–4 dated signals ending at "now"; the insight in a
band below. Use for a trend-led pitch.

**C. Two-brand comparison** — the category convention versus the audience's
unmet need, side by side on two axes. Use when the category is crowded.

## Selection

A for a brand refresh; B for a cultural/seasonal launch; C for a repositioning.
Do not use all three; one proof is enough at this stage.

## Type and density

Medium. Insight 24–32 px; observations 12–14 px. Keep the page quieter than the
concept page; it is the setup, not the punchline.

## Evidence / caption

Every external observation gets a source and date. Audience quotes are real and
attributed (role + date), or clearly labelled as a hypothesis. No invented
statistics.

## Do / Don't

Do: state the tension in one sentence; show the audience's words; name the
window.
Don't: open with a market-size chart; use "young people love…" without
evidence; blame the client's previous agency.
