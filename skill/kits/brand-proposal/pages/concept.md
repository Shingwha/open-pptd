# Page recipe · concept

**Purpose:** land the idea in one sentence and show why it is inevitable.

**Reader task:** repeat the idea after one look and believe it fits the brand.

## Slots

1. Concept line: one sentence, 40–64 px (or 32–40 px for a long line).
2. Three pillars: the behaviors that make the idea real (not adjectives).
3. Visual rationale: how the idea becomes type, image, color, or motion.
4. Optional proof-of-concept image or sketch, labelled as a sketch.
5. One-line brand fit.

## Variants

**A. One statement** — the sentence dominates; three pillars in a narrow strip
below. The default.

**B. Statement + specimen** — left: statement and pillars; right: one image or
type specimen that embodies it.

**C. Manifesto page** — 3–4 short lines of manifesto copy, with the concept
repeated small as a label. Use only if the copy is genuinely good.

## Selection

A for a clear idea; B when the visual system is the sell; C for a culture-led
brand with strong voice. Never combine A and C.

## Type and density

The loudest page in the deck after the cover. Body copy is subordinate. If the
statement is longer than two lines at 48 px, shorten it.

## Evidence / caption

The concept is an argument, not a citation. If a reference image is used, credit
it and label it "reference, not final". Sketches are labelled as sketches.

## Do / Don't

Do: write the idea as a human sentence; make each pillar observable; connect it
to the audience insight.
Don't: use a paragraph; use three abstract adjectives ("bold, human, fresh");
show a moodboard without a decision.
