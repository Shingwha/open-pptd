# Page recipe · proof

**Purpose:** give the jury a reason to believe beyond taste. Proof can be
audience research, a pilot, a benchmark, or a credible precedent.

**Reader task:** see one concrete result or signal that de-risks the direction.

## Slots

1. Title: the proof as a claim ("The concept tested 2.3× higher on recall than
   the current campaign").
2. One proof object: test result, pilot metric, or precedent. One only.
3. Method: sample, stimulus, date, and what was measured (small type).
4. Reading: what it does and does not prove.
5. Caveat: the limit of the evidence.

## Variants

**A. Test result** — one metric with a comparison bar, method below. The
default.

**B. Pilot evidence** — before/after application photos plus one metric.

**C. Precedent** — 2–3 real comparable campaigns with results and sources; no
fake awards.

## Selection

A for a concept test; B for a live pilot; C for a new brand with no test yet.
If there is no proof, say "untested" and propose the test — do not invent one.

## Type and density

Medium-low. The proof number 40–64 px; method 10–12 px. The caveat is visible,
not a footnote.

## Evidence / caption

`Source: <study/agency>, <n>, <date>.` State the stimulus and the population.
For borrowed precedents, cite the campaign, year, and result source; do not
claim the result as yours. Mockups are not results.

## Do / Don't

Do: show the comparison; state the limit; use one proof.
Don't: use a fake testimonial; cite an award you did not win; present a
moodboard as audience validation; hide the method.
