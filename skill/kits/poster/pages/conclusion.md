# Page recipe · conclusion (poster)

**Purpose:** close with what the reader should remember and what follows from
it, in three lines at most.

**Reader task:** leave with one sentence they can repeat at the coffee break.

## Slots

1. Section head: "CONCLUSION" or the takeaway as the head.
2. Two or three conclusion bullets: answer to the question, the size of the
   effect, and the condition under which it holds.
3. One-sentence takeaway, visually the strongest line in the block.
4. Optional implication / next step: what should change or be studied next.
5. Optional limitation: one line, kept honest but not self-defeating.

## Variants

**A. Takeaway band** — a full-width band with the takeaway line and three short
bullets. The default.

**B. Two columns** — "what we found" left, "what it means" right; a hairline
between. Use when the implication is the reason to read.

**C. Single statement** — one sentence at 44–54 px plus a one-line qualification.
Use when the result is one number and the poster has a single claim.

## Selection

A for most posters; B for applied and policy-facing work; C for public-science
posters. Never introduce a new result here; every number must already appear in
the results block.

## Type and density

Takeaway 36–48 px; bullets 26–30 px; limitation 20–24 px. Three bullets maximum.
If the conclusion needs a figure to be understood, the results page did not do
its job — fix that instead of adding a figure here.

## Evidence / caption

The conclusion restates evidence; keep units, n, and conditions identical to the
results block. Hedging language must match the design: say "associated with" if
the study is observational, "reduced" only if it was an intervention.

## Do / Don't

Do: answer the question posed by the title; keep the takeaway quotable; state
the one condition that matters.
Don't: add new claims; repeat the abstract verbatim; end with "thank you" or a
contact block without content; overclaim beyond the design.
