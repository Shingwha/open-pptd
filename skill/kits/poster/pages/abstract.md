# Page recipe · abstract (poster)

**Purpose:** give the one-paragraph version of the work, readable without
walking closer, so the visitor can decide what to ask.

**Reader task:** understand the problem and the claim in under 30 seconds.

## Slots

1. Section head: "ABSTRACT" or a plain-language lead-in, 44–54 px.
2. Problem sentence: what is unknown or broken, one sentence.
3. Approach sentence: what was done, one sentence with the sample/scale.
4. Claim sentence: the result in one sentence, with the key number.
5. Optional implication sentence: why it matters, one sentence.
6. Optional takeaway line: bold or accent treatment, one line.

## Variants

**A. Full-width block** — 100–180 words across two-thirds of the width,
immediately under the title band. The default.

**B. Two-column block** — problem/approach left, claim/implication right, a
hairline between. Use for wide landscape boards.

**C. Claim-first block** — the one-sentence claim set at 40–48 px, the abstract
body below at 26–28 px. Use when the claim is the poster's reason to exist.

## Selection

A for standard research posters; B for landscape boards where the eye moves
sideways; C for public-facing work. Never put the abstract in a narrow side
column — it is the most-read block after the title.

## Type and density

Body 26–30 px, line height 1.45–1.6, maximum width ~90 characters per line.
Short paragraphs beat a wall of text: three to five sentences, no citations in
the abstract — references carry them.

## Evidence / caption

Numbers in the abstract must match the figure and the conclusion exactly: same
unit, same rounding, same n. If the abstract says "preliminary", the results
block must not say "final".

## Do / Don't

Do: state the problem before the method; give one concrete number; keep the
claim sentence short enough to read aloud.
Don't: copy the paper abstract verbatim with citations; use acronyms before
defining them; promise conclusions the results block cannot show; exceed 180
words.
