# Page recipe · references-qr (poster)

**Purpose:** let the reader verify the work and take it away, without asking the
presenter for a link.

**Reader task:** scan the code or copy a reference; leave with the source in
hand.

## Slots

1. Section head: "REFERENCES" (or "REFERENCES & DATA").
2. Five to eight references, one style, 18–20 px, each with authors, year,
   title, venue; DOIs or stable URLs where space allows.
3. QR code: at least 140 px at A2 (200 px preferred), placed at reading height
   in a bottom corner.
4. QR label: one line saying what the code opens (paper, data, code, contact),
   18–20 px.
5. Optional short link in text under the QR for people who cannot scan.
6. Optional funding/acknowledgement line, 16–18 px.

## Variants

**A. Bottom band + QR right** — references across the bottom left, QR and label
in the bottom-right corner. The default.

**B. Right column footer** — references stack under the conclusion in the right
column, QR directly beneath. Use for two-column posters.

**C. QR under the title** — QR top-right beside the venue line, references in
the bottom band. Use in halls where the bottom edge is blocked by furniture.

## Selection

A unless the layout already has a right column; C only when visitors cannot
reach the bottom of the board. Keep the QR at least 72 px inside the trim and
away from the background's busiest area.

## Type and density

References 18–20 px, single line each where possible, hanging indent or a
compact list. Five to eight items: the works the poster actually builds on, not
the paper's full bibliography. Funding line 16–18 px.

## Evidence / caption

Every reference must be real and verifiable — never a placeholder citation. The
QR must resolve to a stable DOI, repository, or PDF; test it on a phone before
printing. A placeholder QR is allowed only when labelled "QR placeholder —
replace with the real code", and it must not look scannable.

## Do / Don't

Do: test the code on two phones; label what it opens; keep one citation style;
put the data/code link next to the paper link when both exist.
Don't: use a shortened URL that expires; paste the whole bibliography; place the
QR on a photo or gradient; use a decorative frame around the code.
