# Page recipe · results (poster)

**Purpose:** make the main evidence the largest object on the page and state
what it shows.

**Reader task:** see the effect and its uncertainty, then carry away one number.

## Slots

1. Section head: "RESULTS" or the finding itself.
2. Main figure or table: 40–55% of the content area, complete and legible.
3. Key-number rail: two or three numbers with unit and label, 80–120 px each.
4. Reading: one to three short lines beside or under the figure — what to look
   at and the effect size.
5. Figure number + caption + source.
6. Optional annotation: one callout at the decisive comparison.

## Variants

**A. Figure centre, numbers in a rail** — the figure spans the middle band; the
key numbers sit in a narrow right or left rail. The default.

**B. Figure + interpretation** — figure on the left 60%, reading and numbers on
the right 40%. Use when the figure needs a sentence to be understood.

**C. Table-led** — a compact comparison table with the decisive row marked.
Use only when the comparison is categorical and a figure would obscure it.

## Selection

A for one strong effect; B for effects that need a caveat; C for
multiple-condition comparisons. Never reduce a complete figure to a thumbnail
to make room for more text — cut text instead.

## Type and density

Key numbers 80–120 px; labels 22–26 px; reading 26–30 px; caption 20–22 px. The
figure must survive at 1.5 m; if the axes do not, it is too small. One main
figure only.

## Evidence / caption

Original figure, `fit: contain`, nothing cropped. Caption: what is plotted,
under which condition, with n. Source: authors, year, panel. Re-plotted values:
"Redrawn from <source>; values verified against Table <n>." Key numbers must
match the figure and the abstract.

## Do / Don't

Do: put the claim in the section head; keep error bars and significance marks
explained; repeat the key number once in the conclusion.
Don't: recolour a scientific encoding to match the palette; overlay text on the
plot area; show a screenshot of a table; add a second figure that competes.
