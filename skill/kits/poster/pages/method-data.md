# Page recipe · method-data (poster)

**Purpose:** let a reader judge how the evidence was produced before they read
the result.

**Reader task:** check the sample, the instrument, and the analysis, and decide
whether the result applies to their case.

## Slots

1. Section head: "METHOD & DATA".
2. Sample: who/what, n, period, setting — one line each, not a paragraph.
3. Instrument / procedure: what was measured and how, in two or three items.
4. Analysis: the model, comparison, or test, with the software/version if it
   matters for reproduction.
5. Optional design diagram: the flow of participants/samples or the pipeline;
   one diagram, labelled.
6. Optional limitations line: one line naming the main threat to validity.

## Variants

**A. Three horizontal blocks** — sample / procedure / analysis as equal blocks
across the width. The default.

**B. Diagram + notes** — a design or pipeline diagram on the left 55%, the three
blocks as compact notes on the right. Use when the design is the contribution.

**C. Timeline strip** — a single horizontal timeline with data collection
points, n at each point, and the analysis window marked. Use for longitudinal
studies.

## Selection

A for survey, experiment, and benchmark posters; B for methods papers and
complex designs; C for cohorts and panels. Keep the method block smaller than
the results block: the visitor reads results first and methods second.

## Type and density

Body 26–30 px; list items one line each where possible; diagram labels 20–24 px.
No more than three blocks and six lines total. A methods appendix belongs in the
QR link, not on the poster.

## Evidence / caption

State n, the unit of analysis, and the period. Instruments keep their real
names and versions. A design diagram may be redrawn and labelled "redrawn"; a
photo of the apparatus is evidence and keeps a credit.

## Do / Don't

Do: give n and period first; name the comparison baseline; keep the same
sample/period wording as the results block.
Don't: turn the method into a paragraph; hide the sample size in a caption; use
an unlabelled pipeline; include a protocol that is not in the linked paper.
