# Page recipe · title (poster)

**Purpose:** put the claim, the authors, and the venue at reading distance so a
visitor knows in five seconds whether to stop.

**Reader task:** read the claim, recognise the field, and decide to step in.

## Slots

1. Venue line: conference/session, poster number, date — 18–22 px, muted,
   letter-spaced.
2. Title: the claim or precise question, 96–120 px, at most two lines.
3. Authors: names, 28–34 px, with the presenting author marked.
4. Affiliations and contact: one line or a compact list, 22–26 px.
5. Optional logo strip: institutional marks only, one line, never larger than
   the authors.
6. Optional key visual: one figure or image that carries the subject, placed to
   the side, not behind the title.

## Variants

**A. Full-width title band** — venue, title, authors stacked across the top
8–12% of the canvas. The default.

**B. Title left, key figure right** — title and authors occupy the left 55%;
the key figure starts at the title's baseline on the right. Use when the figure
is the reason to stop.

**C. Statement title with rule** — a single short claim in very large type, one
hairline rule, authors below. Use only when the claim is genuinely short.

## Selection

A for most conference posters; B when the result is visual; C for public-science
and design posters where the sentence is the product. Never center the title
over a full-bleed image without a scrim.

## Type and density

Title 96–120 px, two lines maximum. Authors 28–34, affiliations 22–26. The title
band holds four text blocks at most. If the title needs more than eight words,
rewrite it; the abstract is where detail belongs.

## Evidence / caption

A key visual on the title band is expression, not evidence: if it shows data,
caption and source it. Logos come from the institution's official file; do not
redraw or recolour them.

## Do / Don't

Do: make the claim specific; mark the presenting author; keep the venue line
accurate; leave 72 px of safe margin.
Don't: lead with a logo; set the title in a decorative face for body-level
legibility; use three lines of title; add the abstract to the title band.
