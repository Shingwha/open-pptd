# Page recipe · executive summary

**Purpose:** give the whole period in one page so a reader can stop here and
still make the decision.

**Reader task:** understand the 3–4 findings, their evidence, and the ask.

## Slots

1. Title: the period's conclusion ("Growth held at 18%, but margin fell on
   discounting").
2. 3–4 findings: each a short claim with one evidence pointer (chart, table,
   page number).
3. Evidence strip or mini-chart: one visual anchor, not four.
4. Decision/ask: what you need from the reader, with owner and date.
5. Source line.

## Variants

**A. Findings list + evidence strip** — four full-width rows, each one claim
and one pointer; a small chart or sparkline at the bottom. The default.

**B. Two-column findings** — left: three findings; right: the decision box and
one KPI. Use when the ask is the point.

**C. One-page memo** — a 28–34 px lead paragraph followed by three bold
sub-claims and a one-line ask. Use for a written-first culture.

## Selection

A for a standard review; B when the meeting must decide something; C when the
deck doubles as a pre-read. Do not repeat the cover's KPI row here.

## Type and density

Medium-high. Claims 17–20 px bold; evidence pointers 11–12 px; body 14–16 px.
Keep each finding to two lines. The ask gets its own band at the bottom.

## Evidence / caption

Each finding cites the page or chart it rests on (`p. 6`, `Chart 2`). If a
mini-chart is used, caption and source it like any other chart. No new numbers
that are not shown later.

## Do / Don't

Do: make the ask explicit; use the same metric names as the KPI page; keep the
findings mutually exclusive.
Don't: summarize every page; use "various", "several", or "significant"
without a number; turn it into a table of contents.
