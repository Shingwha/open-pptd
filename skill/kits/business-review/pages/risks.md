# Page recipe · risks

**Purpose:** make the threats to the plan explicit, owned, and testable.

**Reader task:** know what could break the plan, how it will be detected, and
who acts.

## Slots

1. Title: the risk conclusion ("Two execution risks threaten the Q3 recovery").
2. Risk list/table: risk, likelihood/impact (or a 2×2 matrix), trigger,
   mitigation, owner.
3. One highlighted risk: the one that changes the decision.
4. Source/assumption line.

## Variants

**A. Risk table** — 4–6 rows: risk, trigger, mitigation, owner. The default
for a management review.

**B. 2×2 matrix** — likelihood × impact with 3–5 labelled risks; mitigation
listed in a narrow column. Use when prioritization is the point.

**C. Narrative risks** — three risks as short paragraphs with a bold trigger
sentence. Use for a small deck or a qualitative review.

## Selection

A when the audience needs operational follow-through; B for a portfolio or
investment review; C for a discussion-led session. Do not put a risk matrix
and a full table on the same page.

## Type and density

Medium-high. Risk names 14–16 px bold; trigger/mitigation 12–14 px; owner
11–12 px. Keep one line per cell; if a risk needs a paragraph, give it its own
page.

## Evidence / caption

Risks are assumptions, not data; label them as such. If the likelihood/impact
scores come from a model, state the basis. No fabricated probability without a
source or an explicit "judgement".

## Do / Don't

Do: define the trigger as an observable event; assign one owner per risk; state
the mitigation budget or dependency.
Don't: list generic risks ("market conditions"); use a red card grid; assign a
risk to a committee; hide the top risk in row five.
