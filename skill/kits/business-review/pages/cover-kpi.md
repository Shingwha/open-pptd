# Page recipe · cover / KPI

**Purpose:** answer "how did the period go?" before the reader turns the page.

**Reader task:** see the headline status, the few numbers that matter, and the
period covered.

## Slots

1. Period and audience (kicker, 11–13 px).
2. Headline: one status sentence, not a section label ("Revenue reached 96% of
   plan; the gap is one delayed renewal").
3. 3–4 KPI callouts: number, label, actual vs target/prior.
4. One-line verdict or the decision needed.
5. Source/date line.

## Variants

**A. KPI row** — title left, three or four KPI columns across the lower half,
separated by hairlines. The default for a periodic review.

**B. Hero number** — one number at 96–140 px carrying the page, with the other
KPIs small beneath. Use when one metric defines the period.

**C. Split status** — left: title and verdict; right: a traffic-light list of
4–5 metrics with actual/target. Use for an operating review.

## Selection

A for a board review with balanced metrics; B for a launch or a single-metric
period; C when the audience needs red/amber/green status immediately.

## Type and density

Low-to-medium. KPI number 40–64 px, label 11–13 px, comparison 11–12 px. The
headline is the only large sentence; KPIs do not compete with it. No more than
four KPIs.

## Evidence / caption

Every KPI states period, unit, and target. `Source: company data, Q2 FY2026`
at the bottom. If the KPI is a forecast, label it. No chart required.

## Do / Don't

Do: put the miss next to the number; keep units visible; use one accent for the
exception.
Don't: open with an agenda; use five KPIs; hide the period; decorate with
icons or a stock skyline.
