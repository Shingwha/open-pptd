# Page recipe · trend / drivers

**Purpose:** explain the shape of the period — what moved, when, and why.

**Reader task:** see the trend, the inflection, and the drivers behind it.

## Slots

1. Title: the trend as a conclusion ("Renewals fell after the March price
   change; new logos did not compensate").
2. Chart: one main time series, 45–60% of the content area.
3. Driver list: 2–3 drivers, each with a number or a short mechanism.
4. Annotation: one callout on the inflection or gap, with a leader line.
5. Source and period line.

## Variants

**A. Chart left, drivers right** — the default. Chart 55%, three driver rows
35%, a reading line under the chart.

**B. Full-width chart + driver strip** — chart 100% width, three drivers in a
narrow strip below. Use for a long time series.

**C. Small multiples** — 2–3 aligned mini-charts for segments, one shared
scale, drivers beside them. Use when the segment comparison is the point.

## Selection

A for a single business; B when the series is dense; C for a portfolio review.
Never use two different time axes in one page.

## Type and density

Chart labels 10–12 px; driver claims 15–17 px; body 14–16 px. Remove vertical
grid lines; keep faint horizontal lines. The chart must share a baseline with
its reading column.

## Evidence / caption

`Chart 1. <metric> by <period>, <unit>; dashed = forecast.` Source below. State
the measurement basis (gross vs net, recognized vs booked). Annotate only the
inflection that supports the title.

## Do / Don't

Do: mark actual vs forecast with line style; show the prior period; put the
driver's number next to it.
Don't: use a 3-D chart; use default Office colors; start the value axis above
zero without saying so; attribute cause without evidence.
