# Page recipe · segment

**Purpose:** show where the period's result came from — business unit, region,
product, or channel.

**Reader task:** see which segment carried or dragged the result, and by how
much.

## Slots

1. Title: the segment conclusion ("North America carried growth; EMEA renewal
   slipped for a second quarter").
2. Comparison object: grouped bar chart, small multiples, or a table of
   actual/target/prior.
3. Segment notes: one line per segment with a cause or a next step.
4. Total/consolidation line: how the segments reconcile to the company number.
5. Source line.

## Variants

**A. Grouped bar + notes** — segments on the category axis, two series
(actual/target), notes right. The default.

**B. Segment table** — rows = segments, columns = actual, target, variance,
status. Use when precision matters more than shape.

**C. Small multiples** — one mini trend per segment, 3–4 panels, shared scale,
with a one-line takeaway each. Use for a portfolio.

## Selection

A for 3–6 segments; B for 6+ segments or a finance audience; C when the trend
differs by segment. Do not mix chart and table for the same data on one page.

## Type and density

High density is fine. Table cells 12–14 px; segment notes 13–15 px; chart
labels 10–12 px. Keep the segment order identical across pages.

## Evidence / caption

`Chart 2. Revenue by segment, Q2 FY2026 vs target (USD m).` Source below. Say
whether the total includes unallocated/other. If segments were redefined, note
it and point to the appendix.

## Do / Don't

Do: reconcile segments to the total; show variance, not just actuals; use one
highlight for the segment that changes the decision.
Don't: use a pie chart for six segments; sort by size if the story is rank;
omit the "other" row; use two color scales.
