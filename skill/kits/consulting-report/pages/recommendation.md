# Page recipe · recommendation

**Purpose:** convert the analysis into a decision the client can act on.

**Reader task:** know what to do, why, what it costs, and what could go wrong.

## Slots

1. Title: the recommendation as a decision ("Approve the partnership; run a
   12-month pilot before full entry").
2. Rationale: 3 reasons tied to the evidence and the criteria.
3. What it takes: budget, people, capabilities, dependencies.
4. Risks and mitigations: 2–3, each with a trigger and owner.
5. Conditions and kill criteria: what must hold, and when to stop.
6. Ask: the approval and date.

## Variants

**A. Decision memo** — recommendation + three reasons + conditions, in one
reading column. The default.

**B. Recommendation + risk ledger** — left: the recommendation; right: risks,
triggers, mitigations. Use for a risk-averse board.

**C. Choice architecture** — the recommendation at the top, two rejected
options below with one-line reasons. Use when the client saw the matrix.

## Selection

A for a standard approval; B when execution risk is the objection; C when the
audience needs to see the alternatives were taken seriously.

## Type and density

Medium. Recommendation 24–30 px; reasons 15–17 px; risks 12–14 px. Keep the
conditions visible — do not bury them in an appendix.

## Evidence / caption

Reference the exhibits by number. Costs and benefits carry a source or an
explicit estimate method. The recommendation page may summarize numbers from
earlier pages; it must not introduce new unexplained data.

## Do / Don't

Do: state the decision, the cost, the risk, and the stop condition.
Don't: recommend "further analysis" as the answer; hide the cost; list risks
without owners; use a motivational closing image.
