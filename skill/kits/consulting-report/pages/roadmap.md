# Page recipe · roadmap / risks

**Purpose:** show how the recommendation becomes real, and what would stop it.

**Reader task:** see the sequence, owners, milestones, and the kill criteria.

## Slots

1. Title: the execution claim ("Three phases over 12 months; the pilot gate is
   the only hard stop").
2. Phases: 3–4 phases on a horizontal time axis, with duration and outcome.
3. Milestones/gates: 2–3 decision gates, with the criterion for passing.
4. Owners and dependencies: one owner per phase; cross-team dependency marked.
5. Risks: 2–3 execution risks with trigger and mitigation.
6. Source/assumption line.

## Variants

**A. Phase bar** — a horizontal 12-month axis with three phase blocks above and
gates below. The default.

**B. Milestone ladder** — vertical sequence of 4–5 milestones with dates and
owners. Use for a short program.

**C. Roadmap + risk ledger** — timeline on top, risk table below. Use when the
board will ask "what can go wrong?" immediately.

## Selection

A for a multi-phase program; B for a single workstream; C for a risk-sensitive
approval. Do not use a Gantt chart with dozens of tasks — this is a decision
deck, not a project plan.

## Type and density

Medium-high. Phase names 14–16 px; dates/milestones 10–12 px; risk cells
11–13 px. Keep the time axis to one scale and one direction. Use absolute
dates.

## Evidence / caption

Effort and duration are estimates: label the basis (benchmark, vendor quote,
team estimate). Gates need a measurable criterion. Source line for any external
benchmark.

## Do / Don't

Do: show the pilot gate and the kill criterion; name one owner per phase; mark
the critical dependency.
Don't: use a 30-task Gantt; assign phases to "the team"; use a roadmap without
dates; hide the risk table in the appendix.
