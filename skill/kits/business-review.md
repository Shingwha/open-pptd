# Kit · business-review

**System:** `executive` (see `../references/design.md`).
**Theme and layout:** `../references/themes.md` → Business Review.
**Audience:** managers, board, operating team, cross-functional stakeholders.
**Reader task:** grasp how the period went, why, what is at risk, and who does
what next — fast.

## Narrative

```
cover/KPI → executive summary → trend and drivers → segment performance →
risks → actions/owners/dates → appendix and sources
```

The titles alone must tell the story. A reader flipping only titles should
understand the period, the cause, and the ask. Put the single most important
status line on the cover; do not open with an agenda.

## Core page types

| Page | Job |
|---|---|
| Cover / KPI | Period, headline status, 3–4 KPI callouts, one-line verdict. |
| Executive summary | 3–4 findings with evidence pointers, plus the decision needed. |
| Trend / drivers | The main time series and the 2–3 drivers that explain it. |
| Segment | Business unit, region, or product split compared with target/prior. |
| Risks | Threats to plan, with observable trigger, mitigation, and one owner. |
| Actions | Action, owner, absolute due date, status, and measure of done. |
| Appendix / sources | KPI formulas, source systems, restatements, sources, and backup exhibits. |

Optional: a section divider for a long deck, a `what changed since last review`
page for a returning audience, or customer/ops detail when it drives a decision.

## Content density

- Body pages are dense with charts, tables, and readings; covers and the closing
  action page may breathe. Use the type and geometry rules in `themes.md`.
- Keep at most four KPIs, four summary findings, three principal drivers, six
  risks, and six actions on their respective pages. Split or cut beyond that.
- Keep stable title/evidence/source/page-number axes; vary the body between
  chart-led, table-led, matrix-led, and commitment-led compositions.

## Evidence rules

- Every number has a period, unit, and source. Internal data is labelled
  `company data`; external data gets a report name and date.
- Show target or prior period alongside actuals. A number without a baseline is
  not a result. Keep the measurement basis visible (gross/net, booked/recognized).
- Keep KPI definitions identical across pages. Changed definitions and
  restatements name old/new values, affected period, source system, and owner in
  the appendix.
- Forecasts are labelled as forecasts with assumptions. Distinguish actual from
  forecast by line style as well as text.
- Segment values reconcile to the total, including unallocated/other. Segment
  order and definitions stay consistent across pages.
- Risks are assumptions or judgements unless a sourced model says otherwise;
  never fabricate probability. Actions depending on forecasts state the basis.
- Never invent a market size, benchmark, customer quote, owner, or due date.

## Do

- Make titles conclusions with subject, verb, and number when available.
- Put the `so what` beside the chart, not in a paragraph below it.
- Give each finding an evidence pointer and each action an observable measure.
- Close with the decision or resource needed, not a summary of the deck.

## Don't

- Don't use section labels as titles (`Business Review`, `Q2 Performance`).
- Don't build decorative card grids or use gradients, shadows, and filler icons.
- Don't hide a miss in a footnote or silently change a metric definition.
- Don't use an agenda unless the deck is long enough to need navigation.

## Anti-homogenization

- The narrative is an argument, not a template. Drop empty sections and split
  the section carrying the decision.
- No two consecutive pages share a composition. Use at least four layout
  grammars in a ten-page deck and never run three KPI pages in a row.
- Adapt the proportional guidance in `themes.md`; do not assemble a fixed deck
  or reuse a composition merely to fill its regions.
