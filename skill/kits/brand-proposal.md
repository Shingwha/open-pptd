# Kit · brand-proposal

**System:** `editorial` (see `../references/design.md`).
**Theme and layout:** `../references/themes.md` → Brand Proposal.
**Audience:** brand owner, marketing lead, creative jury, client decision maker.
**Reader task:** believe the concept, see the system, and approve the direction.

## Narrative

```
cover → opportunity/insight → concept → visual system → campaign/applications
→ proof → closing/ask
```

The deck sells a point of view. Every loud page must be followed by a page that
proves or explains. One concept, one visual rule, one motif — consistency is the
craft.

## Core page types

| Page | Job |
|---|---|
| Cover | Subject, proposal type, author/date, and one strong visual anchor. |
| Opportunity | Cultural/business opening, audience insight, brand problem, why now. |
| Concept | One memorable idea sentence, three observable pillars, visual rationale. |
| Visual system | Wordmark, palette, type, grid, imagery, motion, and misuse rules. |
| Campaign / applications | The idea across 2–3 real formats and touchpoints. |
| Proof | Audience test, benchmark, pilot, or credible precedent with method/caveat. |
| Closing / ask | Restate concept, one explicit ask, next step/date, contact/credits. |

Optional: team/credits, timeline, budget, and production appendix. Keep the pitch
on the idea; production detail goes last.

## Content contracts and density

- The cover needs one title, one metadata block, and one image/field — not a
  paragraph explaining the concept.
- Opportunity pages use 2–3 sourced observations and one audience insight. Real
  audience quotes carry role/date; otherwise label the insight as a hypothesis.
- Concept pillars are behaviors, not adjectives. Reference images and sketches
  are labelled as such; a moodboard is not a decision.
- Visual-system pages show at most six colors and two type families, with at
  least one real application and one misuse. Accessibility claims need contrast
  values, not adjectives.
- Applications show at most three formats with a clear hierarchy. State what
  stays fixed and what can flex across channels.
- Proof uses one object and shows method, population/sample, stimulus, date,
  comparison, and limitation. If no proof exists, say `untested` and propose a
  test.
- Closing asks for one decision and echoes the cover. No new idea or evidence.

## Evidence rules

- Brand assets (logo, VI, product shots) come from the user or official source;
  never redraw or generate them.
- Audience/test data is evidence: cite method, n, population, and date. No fake
  quotes, awards, case results, or client logos.
- Mockups are labelled as mockups and are not performance proof. Borrowed
  precedents keep campaign, year, result source, and ownership clear.
- Generated imagery is expression only, with consent and provenance recorded in
  `assets-prompts.md`. Never generate a person as a testimonial or a product as
  fact.

## Do

- Show the system on real formats, not abstract swatches alone.
- Let type, scale, and whitespace do the work.
- Alternate loud and quiet pages; the quiet pages make the loud ones work.
- End with a clear gate: approve, fund, pilot, or proceed.

## Don't

- Don't use generic AI gradients, glassmorphism, rounded card stacks, or blobs.
- Don't mix two visual languages, motifs, or decorative accent colors.
- Don't use stock photos as evidence or fake a real-world placement as fact.
- Don't apply editorial styling to a business review or academic deck.

## Anti-homogenization

- Drop or reorder pages when the pitch logic needs it; no fixed deck assembly.
- No two consecutive pages share a composition; never run three full-bleed
  statement pages in a row.
- Use at least four layout grammars in a ten-page deck. Adapt the proportional
  directions in `themes.md` rather than repeating a fixed specimen or hero page.
