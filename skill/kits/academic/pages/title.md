# Page recipe · title

**Purpose:** establish the research identity and the one-line claim in under
ten seconds. The reader should know the field, the question, and the speaker.

**Reader task:** decide whether this talk is relevant and credible.

## Slots

1. Kicker: venue, occasion, or "PhD defense" (12–14 px, letter-spaced, muted).
2. Title: the claim or precise topic (48–64 px, 2–3 lines maximum).
3. Subtitle: the method or scope in one line (18–20 px, muted).
4. Author block: name, affiliation, co-authors, date (13–15 px).
5. One visual anchor: a solid color field, a full-bleed research image, or a
   single large figure detail. Not a decorative pattern.

## Variants

**A. Field + paper** — title left, a narrow full-height primary field on the
right; author block bottom-left; institution line small. Strict, Swiss.

**B. Full-bleed evidence** — a research image or material photo covers the
page; a solid block holds the title and author text. Title never sits directly
on a busy image without an opaque block.

**C. Quiet title page** — white/ivory page, oversized title, one hairline
between title and author block, small figure detail at the lower right at
25–35% width. The most formal option.

## Selection

- Quantitative/engineering: A or B.
- Humanities/archival: B or C with a document detail.
- If the deck opens with a dark cover, close with the same field proportions.

## Type and density

Low density. The title is the only large object. Keep the author block within
one text box; do not scatter five small labels around the page. Leave at least
one margin wider than the others.

## Evidence / caption

If a figure detail is used on the cover, caption it in the delivery notes or as
a small source line; do not imply it is the main result. No source line is
needed for a pure title page.

## Do / Don't

Do: state the field and the claim; align title and author block to one spine.
Don't: put the institution emblem at 40% opacity as a watermark; use a
gradient; center a paragraph; list six co-authors at 9 px.
