# Page recipe · question

**Purpose:** turn the gap into a testable question or hypothesis and define its
boundaries. This page is the contract for the rest of the deck.

**Reader task:** know exactly what will be tested and what would count as an
answer.

## Slots

1. Title: the question, not "Research Questions".
2. Question block: one primary question (28–36 px), optionally 2–3
   sub-questions at body size.
3. Hypothesis (if confirmatory): directional statement, clearly labelled.
4. Definitions: key constructs and how they are operationalized (small type).
5. Scope: sample/system, period, and exclusions.

## Variants

**A. One big question** — a single 32–40 px question occupying the upper half,
definitions and scope in a narrow lower band. The strongest option for a
defense.

**B. Question + hypothesis pair** — left: question; right: hypothesis and the
prediction it implies. Use a hairline, not two cards.

**C. Question ladder** — RQ1 → RQ2 → RQ3 as a numbered sequence with the
analysis mapped to each. Use when the paper has multiple studies.

## Selection

A for a single-study thesis; B for experimental work with a directional
prediction; C for a multi-study paper. Never present a topic ("X in Y") as the
question.

## Type and density

Low-to-medium. Question 28–40 px; body 16–18 px; definitions 12–13 px. Keep one
focal question; sub-questions are subordinate by size and position, not by
color.

## Evidence / caption

No chart is required. If the question references a prior result, cite it. A
conceptual diagram is allowed only if it clarifies the constructs; label it as
a framework, not data.

## Do / Don't

Do: use verbs ("does X predict Y?"); state what would falsify the hypothesis.
Don't: ask three unrelated questions; hide the operational definition in an
appendix; use a stock photo of a laboratory.
