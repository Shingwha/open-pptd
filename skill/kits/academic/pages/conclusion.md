# Page recipe · conclusion

**Purpose:** answer the question and state the contribution in a form the
reader can repeat after leaving the room.

**Reader task:** know what was found, what it means, and what comes next.

## Slots

1. Title: "Answer" or the answer itself ("X explains most of the effect; Y
   does not").
2. Answers: one short numbered block per research question, each pointing back
   to the evidence (figure/table/section number).
3. Contribution: 1–2 lines on what is new.
4. Implication or next step: one line.
5. Closing line: "Questions" or the final claim — not "Thank you" alone.

## Variants

**A. Numbered answers** — 2–4 rows, each with an index numeral, the answer, and
an evidence pointer. The default.

**B. One-sentence close** — a single 32–40 px claim plus three small evidence
markers below. Use when the deck has one finding.

**C. Return to the cover** — reuse the cover's field proportions and type
composition, replacing the title with the answer. Closes the visual loop.

## Selection

A for multi-question work; B for a single decisive result; C when the cover had
a strong composition and the deck is short. Never close with a bare "Thank
you" page that has no answer.

## Type and density

Low density. Answers 18–22 px; index numerals 28–36 px in primary; evidence
pointers 11–12 px muted. Leave a clear zone at the bottom for the closing line
or contact info.

## Evidence / caption

Each answer points to the evidence already shown (`Fig. 2`, `Table 1`, `§4.2`).
Do not introduce new numbers here. If the conclusion includes a limitation,
restate it in one clause rather than re-opening the discussion.

## Do / Don't

Do: answer the exact question asked; use the same terminology as the question
page; give the audience one sentence to remember.
Don't: summarize every page; exaggerate the scope; add a new citation; end with
a generic stock photo.
