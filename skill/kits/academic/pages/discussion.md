# Page recipe · discussion

**Purpose:** interpret the evidence and bound the claim. This is where a
committee decides whether the author understands their own result.

**Reader task:** see the explanation, the alternatives, and the limits without
defensiveness.

## Slots

1. Title: the interpretation as one sentence ("The effect is consistent with
   X, but not with Y").
2. Explanation: the mechanism or account, with the evidence that supports it.
3. Alternatives: 2–3 rival explanations and why the design weakens them.
4. Limitations: sample, measure, design, or scope — each with its direction of
   bias.
5. Contribution: what is now known that was not known before (one line).
6. Next step (optional): the study that would settle the remaining question.

## Variants

**A. Claim · support · rival** — three stacked bands: interpretation, support,
rivals. Use hairlines between bands.

**B. Two-column ledger** — left: "supported by"; right: "not settled by"; a
single vertical rule. The most honest layout for mixed results.

**C. Limitation matrix** — a small table (limitation, affected claim,
direction of bias, mitigation). Use for methods-heavy work.

## Selection

A for a clean result; B for mixed or surprising results; C when the committee
will interrogate validity. Do not use C to dump a list of generic limitations.

## Type and density

Medium. Body 16–18 px; rival/limitation items 15–17 px. Keep each item to two
lines. The limitations section must not look smaller or grayer than the
contribution — it is part of the argument.

## Evidence / caption

Reference the figure/table numbers shown earlier; no new evidence is introduced
here unless it is a robustness check (then caption and source it). No stock
imagery.

## Do / Don't

Do: connect each alternative to a specific result; state the direction of bias.
Don't: write "limitations include sample size" with no consequence; introduce a
new result in the discussion; blame reviewers; use a decorative quote.
