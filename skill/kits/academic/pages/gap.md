# Page recipe · gap

**Purpose:** make the missing knowledge explicit. This is not a literature
review; it is the shortest path from "what is known" to "what is not".

**Reader task:** accept that the gap is real, important, and unaddressed.

## Slots

1. Title: the gap as a claim ("No study has separated X from Y at Z scale").
2. Known: 2–3 established findings, each with a citation marker.
3. Unknown: the specific missing evidence, set apart (larger type or a
   hairline-delimited block — not a card).
4. Why it matters: one sentence on consequence for theory, practice, or method.
5. Scope: population, period, or system boundaries (small type).

## Variants

**A. Two-column known/unknown** — left: established findings as a short list;
right: the gap in 24–28 px with a thin vertical rule between. Good for a
science audience.

**B. Timeline of evidence** — horizontal years with 3–5 milestone studies and a
terminal empty segment labelled "this work". Use a native line/shape sequence,
not a decorative curve.

**C. Single-paragraph argument** — one 24–28 px paragraph carrying known →
unknown → why, with citations as superscripts and a small scope line. Best for
humanities and short decks.

## Selection

Use A when the reader needs the literature positions; B when the gap is
historical or technical; C when the deck is narrative and short. Do not stack
all three.

## Type and density

Body 17–19 px; the unknown block 22–28 px; citations 10–11 px. Leave the lower
third lighter than the upper two-thirds. No more than five citations on this
page; a full reference list belongs on the references page.

## Evidence / caption

Every cited claim carries an author–year marker matching the references page.
If a figure is used to show the gap (e.g. coverage map), give it a caption and
source; do not use a decorative photo.

## Do / Don't

Do: name the gap in one sentence a non-specialist can repeat.
Don't: write "many studies have explored…" with no names; use a card grid of
papers; imply the gap is new without checking; cite a source you have not read.
