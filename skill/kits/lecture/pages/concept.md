# Page recipe · concept

**Purpose:** introduce one idea with its definition, its shape, and the
misconception it replaces.

**Reader task:** say what the idea is, recognise it in an example, and stop
applying the old mental model.

## Slots

1. Kicker: segment and topic.
2. Title: the idea stated as a claim, 32–40 px.
3. Definition: one sentence, 20–24 px, no nested clauses.
4. Diagram: the structure, process, or relationship; 35–55% of the content
   area. A concept map, a labelled axis, or a three-part flow — not decoration.
5. Key term + its precise wording, in the deck's own language.
6. Misconception: "not this — this" with one line of why it matters.
7. Optional source for a borrowed definition.

## Variants

**A. Definition + diagram** — definition left, diagram right; misconception in
a bottom strip. The default.

**B. Term + contrast table** — the term and two or three contrasting cases in a
small table; diagram omitted. Use for classification concepts.

**C. Misconception repair** — the wrong model first (visibly marked), then why
it fails, then the correct model. Use only when the audience reliably arrives
with the wrong model.

## Selection

A for most concepts; B for taxonomies and definitions that are only clear by
contrast; C when prior teaching has installed the wrong model. Never stack A and
C on one page.

## Type and density

Title 32–40 px; definition 20–24 px; diagram labels 14–18 px; misconception
16–18 px; source 11–13 px. One concept per page. If the diagram needs a legend
of more than three items, the diagram is too complex — split it.

## Evidence / caption

Redrawn diagrams are allowed and encouraged; label them "redrawn from <source>"
when they follow a published figure. Quotes and definitions keep the original
wording and a source line. Numbers in the diagram keep units.

## Do / Don't

Do: define in one sentence before showing the diagram; name the misconception
explicitly; keep diagram labels in the same words used in the example.
Don't: introduce two new terms on the same page; use an unlabelled arrow
diagram; rely on colour alone to carry meaning; quote a definition without a
source.
