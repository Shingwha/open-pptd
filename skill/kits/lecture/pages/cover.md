# Page recipe · cover

**Purpose:** name the session and state the question or capability the lecture
delivers, so a learner knows why to stay.

**Reader task:** decide what this session is about and what they will be able
to do at the end.

## Slots

1. Course or module label: 10–12 px, letter-spaced, muted.
2. Lecture title: a question or a promise, 52–64 px, at most two lines.
3. One-line scope: the topic boundary and the level ("introductory", "for
   practitioners with basic statistics").
4. Instructor, affiliation, session/date.
5. Optional visual anchor: one diagram or a single real object; never a
   decorative stock photo.

## Variants

**A. Title left, schedule strip** — title and scope on the left 60%; the session
date, room, and instructor in a narrow bottom strip. The default.

**B. Question-led full width** — the question spans the content width in two
lines; scope and instructor below. Use when the lecture is organised around one
question.

**C. Dark cover** — a solid dark field with the title in white and one accent
mark for the session number. Use only when the room is dark and the deck is
short; the following pages return to light.

## Selection

A for standard course lectures; B for seminars and single-question talks; C for
evening/workshop slots. Never combine C with a light closing page.

## Type and density

Title 52–64 px, scope 17–20 px, metadata 11–13 px. The cover carries at most
four text blocks. If the title needs three lines, shorten it — it is a promise,
not an abstract.

## Evidence / caption

No evidence on the cover. If a real object or site is shown, credit the source
in 10–11 px; label a redrawn diagram as redrawn.

## Do / Don't

Do: write the title as the question the lecture answers; state the level; keep
the date and room accurate.
Don't: paste the course syllabus; use a stock campus photo; put the outline on
the cover; set the title over a busy image without a scrim.
