# Page recipe · summary

**Purpose:** consolidate the session into what the learner keeps, and point to
the next step.

**Reader task:** leave able to repeat the three key points and knowing what to do
next.

## Slots

1. Kicker: "SUMMARY" plus the session number.
2. Title: the one-sentence takeaway, 30–36 px.
3. Three takeaways: the concept, the move, the check — each one line.
4. Optional concept map: the session's terms connected; no new nodes.
5. Next step: the reading, exercise, or the next session's question.
6. Optional exit ticket: one question the learner answers before leaving.
7. Source line when the summary quotes a definition or a number.

## Variants

**A. Three-point recap** — title plus three numbered takeaways and a next-step
line. The default.

**B. Concept map** — the session's terms in a small map with the takeaway as its
caption. Use when the session introduced many related terms.

**C. Exit ticket** — one question and a blank answer area, plus a one-line
recap. Use at the end of a workshop or a live class.

## Selection

A for most lectures; B for terminology-heavy sessions; C when the instructor
needs feedback. Never introduce new content on this page — a summary that adds
a fourth idea is a new concept page.

## Type and density

Title 30–36 px; takeaways 20–24 px; next step 16–18 px; source 11–13 px. Three
takeaways, not seven. If the session had four objectives, map each to a
takeaway or say which was deferred.

## Evidence / caption

Summaries repeat evidence already shown; keep the unit and the condition. If the
takeaway is a number, repeat its source line. Do not round a result into a
stronger claim on the summary page.

## Do / Don't

Do: make the takeaway sentence memorable; map takeaways back to the objectives;
state the next step concretely.
Don't: paste the agenda with checkmarks; add a thank-you page with no content;
introduce a new term; end with "any questions" as the only content.
